# A19 SPH liquid kernel — 2026-10-27

## Kernel

New `src/sph.js`, zero-dependency, Müller-style SPH:

- Poly6 density kernel, spiky pressure gradient, viscosity Laplacian —
  exported individually so tests can verify them against closed forms and
  brute-force sums.
- `calibrateMass(spacing, h, restDensity)`: mass that makes a cubic lattice
  sit exactly at rest density.
- Uniform-grid neighbor search; densities/pressures per step; symmetric
  pressure accelerations; box bounds with restitution.

## Formulation bug caught by the momentum test

The first version computed Müller's sums as "forces" and then divided by
particle density in the integrator. Those sums are already accelerations
(the 1/ρ factors live inside the symmetric pressure term); the extra
division broke momentum conservation measurably and softened pressure
exactly where compression was worst. The kernel now exposes
`computeSPHAccelerations`, documents the invariant, and the momentum test
guards it. Related honesty note: Müller's viscosity term is not
momentum-antisymmetric when densities differ — the exact-conservation test
therefore runs pressure-only, and the code says so.

## Verified (tests/sph.test.mjs, 6/6)

- Kernel values match analytic forms (including W(0) = 315/(64πh³)).
- Hash-grid densities equal brute-force O(n²) densities to 1e-10 on a
  jittered lattice.
- Calibrated lattice at ρ0 = 1000: interior density within 1%, pressure
  near zero, net interior acceleration < 1e-6 (symmetry cancellation).
- Isolated pressure-only system: total momentum conserved to 1e-9 over 100
  steps; center of mass travels uniformly to 1e-6.
- Over-compressed lattice (density > 1.5 ρ0) expands under its own pressure.
- Pool collapse: contained at all times, running max density < 2 ρ0, mean
  kinetic energy dissipates to < 5% of peak, centroid collapses.

Known limitation, stated in the test: without boundary particles the pool
settles into a floor layer rather than a deep stacked pool — the oracles
assert safety/energy behavior, not stacking quality.
