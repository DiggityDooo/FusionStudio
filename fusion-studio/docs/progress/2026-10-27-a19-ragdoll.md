## Solver hardening found by this slice (in `src/rigid-body.js`)

The ragdoll was a brutal integration test for the rigid solver; each defect was measured before fixing:

1. Single-pass rods stretched 12.7 cm → constraint iterations added.
2. Rods iterated without ground still broke (7.3 cm) → ground contact interleaved into the relaxation loop.
3. Position-only projection pumped energy (278 J perpetual swing) → velocity-level rod solve added.
4. Rod-supported bodies kept phantom gravity velocity (settled pile "gliding" at 0.5 m/s, never sleeping) → PBD velocity update from positional corrections, restricted to airborne Y after wider variants ejected wedged spheres (54–314 J spikes).
5. Friction ran before the velocity update, ratcheting a leaning pile sideways at 0.22 m/s → post-solve contact velocity pass (bounce-safe downward-velocity zeroing + friction last).
6. Adjacent segment spheres overlap at build (centers 0.19 m, radii sum 0.32) and pair resolution fought the rods from step 0 → rod-connected pairs excluded from self-collision (standard practice; real ragdolls use capsules for the same reason).
7. Sleeping bodies were dragged under the floor by rod/pair solves → sleeping counts as infinite mass in all constraint solves.
8. Sphere contacts have no rolling resistance → opt-in exponential damping in `stepWorld` (default 0; analytic rigid-body oracles unaffected), 0.5/s for the ragdoll.

Final measured behavior: drop settles to KE ≈ 0.1–0.3 J with rod error ~1e-4 m and no COM drift over 5 s.
